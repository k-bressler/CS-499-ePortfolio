Professional Self-Assessment

Academic Journey

I started my academic journey at Southern New Hampshire University in January 2024, and now, after almost three years, I will have earned my bachelor's degree in computer science. I completed my associate's degree in general education many years earlier, shortly after high school. However, as I got older, I began to reflect on the things in life that inspired me. I have always enjoyed numbers, puzzles, math, and problem-solving in a creative but functional manner. These interests ultimately led me to pursue a degree in computer science.

Throughout the program, I was able to build on my existing skills while developing new ones in software development, data management, and the processes involved in creating successful computing solutions. When I began the program, I assumed most courses would focus primarily on programming. However, I quickly learned there was much more to computer science than simply writing code. Planning, communication, collaboration, testing, and the ability to adapt and develop new solutions are all important aspects of the field. These are skills I have strengthened throughout my academic journey.

As I progressed through the program, I began to find my niche within the broader field of computer science: data analysis and management. I currently work as a commercial analyst for a real estate evaluation company that operates throughout the United States. Although my position is not specifically within the field of data analytics, it has strengthened my ability to evaluate information, recognize patterns, communicate findings, and develop creative solutions, as not all properties and locations are the same. This experience has complemented my computer science education, allowing me to build on my analytical and data management skills that can be applied across different industries. As I move forward professionally, I hope to transition into a career focused more directly on data analysis or data management.

There are several important aspects of computer science, but two are equally important in everyday professional environments: collaboration and communication. Within computer science, considering the needs of users, stakeholders, and organizations is essential to developing successful solutions. Throughout my coursework, I gained experience in software development planning, system design, and documentation. These experiences strengthened my ability to recognize project requirements and communicate technical information in an organized manner appropriate for different audiences. My courses also introduced software development lifecycles, including Agile and Waterfall methodologies. In my current profession, I regularly collaborate and communicate with team members and management to complete reports in a timely manner while maintaining quality. Additionally, through my capstone, my code review and enhancement narratives demonstrated my ability to communicate technical decisions through oral, written, and visual formats.

Other areas I have strengthened throughout the program include my understanding of software engineering, algorithms, and data structures. During my time at SNHU, I have worked with different programming languages, including Python, Java, and C++, as well as various development environments and problem-solving techniques. These experiences required me to consider not only whether a program functioned, but also how efficiently, reliably, and effectively it performed for its intended users. For example, in one course, I developed an Android application that introduced me to mobile application architecture, user interface design, and local database integration. Another example was an artificial intelligence and machine learning course, which provided opportunities to explore algorithms and how they can evaluate information, recognize patterns, and make informed decisions. Overall, many of my courses challenged me to select and apply appropriate techniques and solutions based on different requirements and problems. My abilities also grew through testing, debugging, and troubleshooting errors, as well as recognizing when changes were necessary to improve functionality.

Data analysis and management became particularly important areas of my education, along with the security considerations that accompany them. Throughout my coursework, I utilized MongoDB, SQL-based databases, and application development tools, gaining experience in data storage, retrieval, and management. These courses taught me that maintaining accurate and consistent information is essential to producing quality data and supporting informed decision-making. My coursework also reinforced the importance of incorporating security into the development process, especially as organizations and individuals become increasingly dependent on information and data. Developing a security-first mindset involves validating user input, implementing proper error handling, protecting sensitive information, and considering potential vulnerabilities before they become problems. These skills are particularly relevant to my interests in data analysis and management, as organizations depend on accurate, accessible, and secure data to support their operations and future goals.

Capstone Reflection

The CS 499 ePortfolio capstone has provided me with an opportunity to showcase the various skills I have acquired by enhancing an existing application. I selected my CS 340 Animal Shelter Dashboard for all three enhancements: software engineering and design, algorithms and data structures, and databases. This allowed me to demonstrate how multiple improvements can be made within one application, building on one another to create a more functional and reliable system. I had also considered some of these improvements while originally completing the CS 340 project, making it a natural choice for my capstone.

The first enhancement focused on software engineering and design through the creation of an animal management interface. This interface expanded the original dashboard by allowing users to add, update, and delete animal records directly through the application. The second enhancement focused on algorithms and data structures, for which I created advanced search, filtering, and sorting options. This improvement allowed users to locate and organize animal records more easily based on multiple criteria. The third and final enhancement focused on databases. For this enhancement, I implemented MongoDB schema validation, improved error handling, and conducted multiple tests to verify the validation functionality. These tests confirmed that valid database operations were successful while invalid operations were rejected. Together, the enhancements demonstrate my ability to evaluate an existing application, identify opportunities for improvement, implement solutions, and verify their functionality.

Overall, my technical abilities, communication skills, and approach to problem-solving have strengthened through my experiences in the computer science program. I have become more confident in my ability to adapt to unfamiliar technologies, troubleshoot issues, evaluate design decisions, and communicate information effectively. I have also gained a greater appreciation for the importance of planning, collaboration, data integrity, and maintaining a security-first mindset throughout the development process. My ePortfolio represents the knowledge, abilities, and skills I have gained, demonstrated through a single practical project.

Although my academic journey is reaching an end, my pursuit of knowledge will not. I intend to continue developing my skills in data analysis and data management as I pursue opportunities within the field. Knowledge and growth should never stop, especially in computer science, where new ideas, technologies, and improvements are constantly evolving.

The following code review examines my original CS 340 Animal Shelter Dashboard and identifies opportunities for improvement in software engineering and design, algorithms and data structures, and databases. The review also discusses the enhancements planned for the final ePortfolio.

Original CS 340 Animal Shelter Dashboard

The original dashboard displays animal shelter records through an interactive data table, pie chart, and map. This application served as the foundation for the three enhancements completed throughout my capstone project.

Enhancement One: Software Engineering and Design

CS 340 – Animal Shelter Dashboard

The artifact selected for this enhancement is an Animal Shelter Dashboard originally developed in CS 340. The application uses Python, Dash, and MongoDB to display and manage animal shelter data.

For the Software Engineering and Design enhancement, I expanded the original dashboard by creating an animal management interface. The enhanced application allows users to add, edit, view, and delete animal records through the user interface. These improvements demonstrate software design, CRUD functionality, input validation, error handling, and integration between the application and MongoDB database.

Enhancement Three: Databases

CS 340 – Animal Shelter Dashboard

For this enhancement, I improved the database functionality of the Animal Shelter Dashboard by implementing MongoDB schema validation, improved error handling, and additional testing.

The database validation enforces required fields, acceptable data types, and restrictions on invalid values. These improvements strengthen data integrity and help prevent invalid records from being inserted or updated.

Seven tests were performed to verify that valid database operations succeeded and invalid operations were rejected.
